# Descriptor cross-check — the 95 pilot molecules against independent free sources

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Run by:** assistant, at Ivan's request · **Status:** accuracy check, not a provenance fix

## Why

The odour descriptors, odour families and note tiers in `data/rows2.json` were **generated by a language
model** in an early session — not supplied by Ivan, not taken from any dataset, not fetched from anywhere.
Confirmed by inspection: no script in the project retrieves descriptors, and they appear already complete
in the oldest artifact (`archive/aroma_index_v1.csv`). The README and DEVLOG previously described them as
"original hand-curation", which overstates their provenance.

This check asks a narrower question than provenance: **are they actually right?** It compares them against
two sources that are free for commercial use, so nothing here creates a licence obligation.

## Sources used

| Source | Licence | Coverage of our 95 |
|---|---|---|
| **HSDB odour text** via PubChem PUG View `Odor` heading | Public domain (US National Library of Medicine) | **51 / 95** |
| **Keller & Vosshall 2016** molecule set (Pyrfume `keller_2016`) | CC BY 4.0 + **CC0** for data | **39 / 95** |
| Either source | — | **58 / 95** |
| **Neither** | — | **37 / 95** |

Retrieved HSDB text is cached verbatim in `data/hsdb_odor.json` with its provenance note.

## Result

Of the 51 molecules with independent HSDB descriptions:

| Outcome | Count |
|---|---|
| Strong agreement (2+ shared odour terms) | 25 |
| Weak agreement (1 shared term) | 24 |
| No shared term | 2 |
| **Direct contradictions** | **0** |

**No descriptor was contradicted by an independent source.** The two "no shared term" cases are artefacts
of crude token matching, not disagreements:

- **Camphor** — ours "camphor, medicinal, cooling"; HSDB "Fragrant and penetrating odor". HSDB simply never
  uses the word *camphor* for camphor. No conflict.
- **Benzyl salicylate** — ours "soft floral, balsamic, powdery"; HSDB "Faint sweet odor". HSDB is vague
  rather than contrary, and benzyl salicylate is genuinely a very soft, faint material.

Most of the "weak" bucket is in fact full agreement that the word-overlap metric under-counts — Limonene
(ours *orange peel, fresh, sweet* / HSDB *"Pleasant lemon-like"*), Muscone (*musk, warm, animalic, powdery* /
*"Powerful soft, sweet animal musk"*), Methyl salicylate (*wintergreen, minty, medicinal* / *"characteristic
odor of wintergreen"*).

### What this does and does not establish

**Does:** where an independent public-domain source exists, the descriptors are accurate. The dataset is
not fabricated, and there is no evidence of error in the corroborable half.

**Does not:**
1. It says nothing about the **37 molecules with no independent source**.
2. It says nothing about **note tiers** — neither source contains them. Tiers remain unverifiable by any
   external data (and a fitted rule reproduces them at only 56% leave-one-out, so they are not derivable
   from vapour pressure and MW either).
3. Agreement is not proof of independence. A model and HSDB may both descend from shared upstream sources.
   This test establishes *correctness*, not *origin*.

## The 37 with no corroboration

They are not a random subset — the pattern is informative. HSDB is a toxicology database of industrial
chemicals; Keller chose chemically diverse commodity molecules. **Fragrance-industry speciality materials
appear in neither.** So the uncorroborated set is essentially the modern synthetic palette, and **24 of the
37 are base notes**:

*Iso E Super · Hedione · Calone · Cashmeran · Habanolide · Ambrettolide · Ethylene brassylate · Civetone ·
Cedramber · Cedryl acetate · Vertofix · Norlimbanol · Agarospirol · Patchoulol · Khusimol · Rotundone ·
Sandalore · Ebanol · Evernyl · Hexyl salicylate · Amyl salicylate · gamma-Undecalactone · Ethyl maltol ·
Aurantiol · Nerolidol · alpha-Isomethyl ionone · beta-Damascone · beta-Damascenone · Helional · Cyclamen
aldehyde · Bourgeonal · Hydroxycitronellal · Undecavertol · Mayol · Rose oxide · Dihydromyrcenol · Melonal*

These are the entries where a human review has the most value, because no free source can ever check them.
Seven already carry a `(verify)` flag from the original session.

## Recommended handling before minting

1. **Relabel honestly** — replace "hand-curation" in README/DEVLOG/site with an accurate description
   (AI-generated, cross-checked against public-domain sources, reviewed by the maintainer).
2. **Review the 37 personally.** That is a bounded, realistic task, unlike reviewing all 95 blind.
3. **Decide the tier question separately** — it is untouched by this check and cannot be outsourced to data.
4. Optionally cite HSDB per molecule for the 51, converting the best-supported half into something with a
   traceable public-domain reference.

## Reproduce

`data/hsdb_odor.json` holds the retrieved text. Source query:
`https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug_view/annotations/heading/JSON?heading=Odor` (3 pages),
matched to our records by PubChem CID. Keller CIDs from
`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyrfume/pyrfume-data/main/keller_2016/molecules.csv`.
